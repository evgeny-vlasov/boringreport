2. US remote AI compute rule takes effect

Entry into force, 19 November · United States / overseas data centers · high confidence

The Bureau of Industry and Security announced the rule on 16 November. It was published and took effect on 19 November, adding licensing controls tied to certain foreign data centers that supply significant remote compute to covered entities. It formally rescinds the earlier AI Diffusion Rule.

Why it matters For covered transactions, who receives computing power can matter alongside where the chip is shipped.

Evidence The Federal Register specifies the end-use control and names five destinations subject to additional chip licensing, with exceptions.

Limits The text includes a transition provision for existing services. Effects on actual compute access require evidence from enforcement and providers.

Watch next Licensing decisions and provider changes. Comments are due 19 January 2027.
